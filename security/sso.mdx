For professional services firms, controlling who has access to client-related workspaces is not optional — it is a professional obligation. Connecting Parle to your identity provider makes Parle access a function of your existing user lifecycle: onboard a new associate in Entra ID or Google Workspace and they can sign in immediately; disable them there and they can no longer authenticate to Parle.

<Note>
  SSO is available on the **Business** plan and above. If a plan is downgraded below Business, SSO sign-in stops working at the next login and users fall back to email/password.
</Note>

## Supported providers

Parle supports the two identity providers most common in professional services firms:

- **Microsoft Entra ID** (formerly Azure Active Directory) — for firms on Microsoft 365
- **Google Workspace** — for firms using Google as their identity layer

## How the connection is configured

SSO is configured by an **Org Admin** under **Org settings → Organization → Single sign-on**. What you supply is deliberately minimal:

| Provider | What you enter | What happens on the provider's side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microsoft Entra ID** | **Tenant ID** and your **email domain**, then enable the toggle | A Global Administrator or Cloud Application Administrator grants Parle consent to sign your users in |
| **Google Workspace** | Your **email domain**, then enable the toggle | A Super Administrator marks Parle's OAuth Client ID as **Trusted** in the Admin console |

Parle does not ask you to register an application and paste back a client secret. You grant consent to the Parle application once, and Parle holds the connection.

## How users are matched

<Warning>
  **Sign-in is routed by email domain, not by account type.** Anyone whose verified email at the provider is on your configured domain can sign in to your organization. Configure the domain your firm actually controls, and only that domain.
</Warning>

- On the login page, a user enters their email address. If the domain matches a configured organization, the **Sign in with Microsoft** or **Sign in with Google** button appears.
- The first time a colleague signs in this way, Parle creates their account automatically and places them in your organization — provided the domain matches and you have an available seat.
- Personal `@gmail.com` addresses cannot sign in to your organization, because `gmail.com` is not your configured domain.

## What changes after SSO is enabled

- **Sign-in flow.** Users authenticate at your identity provider and return to Parle. They never set or use a Parle-specific password.
- **Provisioning.** New users in your identity provider can sign in to Parle without a separate Parle invitation. An Org Admin still controls which projects they are added to.
- **Offboarding.** When you disable, suspend, or delete a user at the identity provider, they can no longer authenticate to Parle. To close out a departing member completely, also deactivate them in Parle — see [Managing Members](/admin/managing-members) — which releases their seat and removes their access to project conversations.
- **Audit trail.** Authentication and org configuration changes are written to the [audit log](/security/audit-log).

## Requiring single sign-on

By default, email and password sign-in stays available alongside SSO. To make SSO the only way in, an **Org Admin** can switch on **Require single sign-on** under **Org settings → Organization → Single sign-on**.

- You need at least one SSO provider enabled first.
- The card shows how many active members do not match an enabled SSO domain, with their email addresses. These are typically personal or external accounts.
- Requiring SSO asks you to confirm. It immediately signs out and blocks the accounts that do not match an enabled domain, and their current sessions cannot be restored.
- Parle refuses the change if it would lock you out of your own organization.
- Select **Stop requiring SSO** on the same card to turn it back off.
